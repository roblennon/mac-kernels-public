# Research statement (2026-11-09)

Written for grant panels and collaborators. Every number here comes from [docs/findings.md](findings.md) or another file in this repo, and I name the source where the number first appears. The register is the same as the findings log: one machine, dated runs, read each result as covering its setup and nothing wider.

Apple GPUs before M5 have large unified memory but no low-precision matrix units. Quantized diffusion checkpoints published for CUDA either fail to load on them or are dequantized on every call. This work keeps INT8-ConvRot, W4A8, W6A8 and GGUF block weights packed and resident, and decodes them in one Metal pass that feeds the stock fp16 GEMM. Exact power-of-two row normalization makes fp16 range-safe for activations that otherwise overflow (MiniMax H3, |x| around 16,000). On an M1 Max the INT8 linear runs within about 2-5% of a resident fp16 GEMM at half the memory, and 1.2-1.9x faster than per-call dequantization. GGUF decode is bit-exact against the reference for twelve block types. End to end: Krea 2 Turbo 512² drops from 8.7 to 3.7 s/step, Qwen-Image 2.1 1024² from 15.1 to 8.8 s/step, and GGUF Q4_K_M samples 1.4-1.7x faster than ComfyUI-GGUF. A 6-bit H3 video model with no stock Apple path runs at 6.5 s/step. The same design gives a frozen-weight backward that saves only codes and scales, within 5-8% of dense fp16 on the large layers. LoRA training on an INT8 base then fits in 9.5 GiB instead of 15.6. An unmerged LoRA side path is off by 0.04% of the output where requantized merging is off by 2.8%. The core changes are submitted to comfy-kitchen, ComfyUI and PyTorch. All results come from one M1 Max with 32 GB. Extending them across Apple generations and memory sizes is the first proposed aim.

## Research question

Can quantized diffusion checkpoints that were built for CUDA run, and be trained on, at near-fp16 speed on Apple GPUs that have no integer matrix units, by keeping the packed weights resident and decoding them inside the pass that feeds the ordinary fp16 GEMM?

The sub-questions I have worked on so far:

1. Does one-pass Metal decode plus the stock fp16 GEMM cost little enough over a resident fp16 weight that the memory saving is free in practice?
2. Can fp16 be made range-safe for the activations these models produce, without changing results?
3. Does the same decode-into-GEMM design give a backward pass cheap enough for LoRA training on a quantized base?
4. Is an unmerged low-rank side path more faithful than merging a LoRA into requantized weights?

## Contributions

1. **A weight-only Metal kernel family for packed formats.** INT8 ConvRot (online rotation), W4A8 and W6A8 weights stay packed in GPU memory and are decoded to normalized fp16 in one pass that feeds the stock GEMM. After hardening, INT8 shapes from Krea 2 run at 39.5, 104.7 and 107 ms against 37.8, 101.4 and 103 ms for a bare fp16 GEMM, so 3.3-4.5% over (findings.md:76-80). The comfy-kitchen PR writeup measures 1.4-2.1%, which sets the 2% lower end in the opening paragraph (findings.md:902). Against ComfyUI's dequantize-per-call path the same kernel is 1.2-1.9x faster at those shapes (76.7, 206.7 and 261.0 ms versus 43.0, 107.2 and 223.3 ms before hardening, findings.md:17-19). INT8 holds the weights at half the fp16 size.
2. **fp16 range safety by exact power-of-two row scaling.** MiniMax H3 text conditioning reaches about 16,000 in magnitude and overflowed fp16 inside the kernel. Dividing each activation row by a power of two before the GEMM and multiplying back in the output dtype fixes it with no mantissa change on the scaling step, at about 10% cost on bf16 inputs (findings.md:58). The fp16 cast after the scaling still rounds to 11 bits; the review's wording caveat is at docs/review/2026-11-09-pre-grant-review.md:63.
3. **Bit-exact GGUF decode on Metal.** The decode is bit-exact against the `gguf` package's reference for all twelve block types the kernel supports. IQ4_NL and IQ4_XS were added after the October 3 write-up, and their 24 bit-exact tests passed on 2026-11-09. The codebook types IQ1, IQ2 and IQ3 are not covered (docs/research/2026-10-03-gguf-on-metal.md:7; vendor/ck-gguf/comfy_kitchen/backends/mps/gguf.py:46-47; vendor/ck-gguf/tests/test_mps_gguf.py:28). On real checkpoints, Qwen-Image Q4_K_M sampling took 39.56 s versus 66.43 s stock (1.68x, findings.md:546) and Wan 2.2 5B Q4_K_M 145.93 s versus 200.71 s (1.38x, findings.md:560; gguf-on-metal.md:130-131).
4. **End-to-end speedups in ComfyUI on an M1 Max.** Krea 2 Turbo at 512²: 8.7 s/step stock to 3.65-3.69 s/step (findings.md:36, :83). Qwen-Image 2.1 at 1024²: 15.1 to 8.8 s/step at 9.1 GB (findings.md:95-96). Wan 2.2 5B at 1280x704x81: 37.4 s/step at 9.1 GB (findings.md:152).
5. **A 6-bit video model with no stock Apple path.** W6A8 pruned MiniMax H3 (16 GB) runs at 6.5 s/step at 480x288x22 in 17.2 GB GPU memory without LoRA; stock ComfyUI cannot run this format on Apple GPUs at all (findings.md:63).
6. **A frozen-weight backward for LoRA training on quantized bases.** `int8_linear_trainable` saves only the int8 codes and scales, computes `dx = dy @ W` in row chunks with per-row power-of-two gradient normalization, and re-rotates ConvRot results (findings.md:372-377). On real Krea 2 Raw layers the backward runs at 9.7 vs 9.0 ms (attn.wq), 25.2 vs 23.9 ms (mlp.up) and 25.9 vs 24.2 ms (mlp.down) against a dense fp16 backward, so 5-8% over on the large layers (findings.md:385, :387-388). The narrow attn.wk layer is the exception at 3.2 vs 2.3 ms, 39% over (findings.md:386). It is about 25% faster than ai-toolkit's whole-layer dequantize per call (findings.md:390). Packed W4 and W6 backward passed 43 MPS tests and a real Qwen mixed-base proof (findings.md:533).
7. **LoRA training on an INT8 base in less memory and time than bf16.** Qwen-Image 2.1 at 512px, 300 steps: 9.46 GiB driver peak and 8.39 s per update on INT8 ConvRot against 15.57 GiB and 12.35 s on bf16, same seed, final loss 0.16436 versus 0.16506 (findings.md:519-523). The log calls this validation loss, but it is computed on two training images, so it is a fixed training-set loss (review:67). The 9.5 and 15.6 GiB figures in the opening paragraph are these two rows rounded. Krea 2 Raw at 512px trained at 12.2 s/step for most steps in 15.9 GB peak (findings.md:404-405). Qwen-Image training at 1024px with query-chunked attention ran at about 36 s/step in 11.49 GiB (findings.md:517).
8. **An unmerged LoRA side path that is more faithful than merging.** On real H3 layers against the exact fp32 merge, the side path is off by 0.04% of the output and the requantized 6-bit merge by 2.8% (findings.md:136). The side path costs about 9% per step on H3 at rank 384 (findings.md:136) and 2.6% on Krea 2 (findings.md:334), and saves the 6.5-minute requantizing merge (findings.md:64).
9. **Upstream submissions.** comfy-kitchen #249 (MPS backend), ComfyUI #16996, #16997, #16998, and PyTorch issue #200782 on the wide-K transposed `F.linear` slowdown (README.md:29-33). At K=32768 the fp16 slowdown is 3.52x and the fp32 slowdown 8.83x (findings.md:512).

## Related work

The format this repo decodes comes from ConvRot ([arXiv 2512.03673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03673), December 2025), which evaluates W4A4 and W8A8 on CUDA and argues that weight-only quantization is insufficient. This work inverts that premise for hardware without integer units: on M1-M4 there is no integer rate advantage to buy, so the weight stays packed and the activation stays fp16 (docs/review/2026-11-09-pre-grant-review.md:115; survey:31). The power-of-two range scaling the kernel uses is the narrow-range arithmetic that Mary and Mikaitis analyze in [SISC 2025](https://perso.lip6.fr/Theo.Mary/doc/NarrowRange.pdf); that paper is the theory behind the fp16 range-safety contribution (review:116). The CUDA counterpart to fusing decode into the GEMM is Ideogram's INT8 kernel ([arXiv 2606.14598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14598), June 2026), and Ideogram's GGUF paper ([arXiv 2606.12280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12280), June 2026) is the only study I know of GGUF quality for diffusion transformers (review:117). On the training side, FourTune ([arXiv 2607.05711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05711), July 2026) is the CUDA frontier for LoRA on a frozen 4-bit base and the first W4A4 trainer; IntLoRA ([arXiv 2410.21759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21759)) is the merge-based alternative to the side path used here (review:118; docs/research/2026-10-02-lora-training-on-quantized-bases.md:19). Ochiai's M3 Ultra study ([arXiv 2605.16259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16259)) found no speedup from quantization on Apple silicon; this repo agrees that quantization does not buy step time on these chips, and adds that it buys memory, and that the stock dequantize-per-call path is what makes it slow (review:119; docs/research/2026-10-02-inference-acceleration-survey.md:20). A torchao maintainer thread ([#4695](https://github.com/pytorch/ao/issues/4695)) reports weight-only INT8 beating ConvRot W8A8 on quality, which points the same way (review:120).

The backward design follows how the existing quantized trainers handle a frozen weight. bitsandbytes' `MatMul4Bit`, torchao's `LinearNF4`, quanto's `QuantizedLinearFunction`, ai-toolkit's ConvRot op and MLX's `QuantizedMatmul::vjp` all compute the input gradient as `grad_output @ dequant(W)` and never a weight gradient; Nunchaku has no training mode at all (lora-training note:15-21). ai-toolkit already trains on MPS over a ConvRot INT8 base, but its MPS branch dequantizes the whole layer weight on each call and fake-quantizes activations to int8 for CUDA deployment (lora-training note:7; findings.md:368). mflux trains Krea 2 Raw as QLoRA q8 on MLX but has no Qwen-Image or Wan trainer (lora-training note:7). LoftQ, PiSSA and QA-LoRA address the starting error or the merge into a quantized base; the note argues they should be avoided for a portable adapter, and that cross-precision transfer of diffusion LoRAs has no published test (lora-training note:67-69).

The October 2 survey placed this kernel work against the other levers on M1-M4. Per-step linear layers sit near the fp16 ALU ceiling, about 9 of 10.4 TFLOPS on the M1 Max, so no custom GEMM, including Metal FlashAttention or an MLX port, beats the stock one by more than a few percent (survey:7, :35, :60). PyTorch 2.13 and later ship a fused simdgroup prefill SDPA that the repo's runs likely already use; FlexAttention on MPS pays only at very sparse masks, and INT8 attention has no integer units to run on before M5 (survey:11-16). The caching family, TeaCache, MagCache, EasyCache, Spectrum and cache-dit, reports gains against 50-step baselines and has nothing to skip at the 4-8 steps these models run (survey:20). Step-distillation LoRAs for Krea 2, Qwen-Image 2.1 and Wan 2.2 5B are the largest remaining lever, and their value on a quantized base depends on applying them without a requantizing merge, which is what motivated the side path (survey:24-27, :31). SVDQuant and Nunchaku's fused low-rank branch is the direct precedent for that side path (survey:31). Tiny VAEs, expert-swap scheduling and the h3.c compute knobs address decode and load time rather than step time (survey:39-45).

Concurrent Apple-side projects mostly target M5 Neural Accelerators and macOS 26.2 MPP paths. ComfyUI-AppleSilicon-FP8 v1.5.0 ships W8A8 that is M5-only and capped at 2048 tokens; Draw Things' 8-bit S and MQA path, MLX #4802 and h3.c's int8 path are all M5-only, with h3.c falling back to bf16 on M3 and older (review:90-95). On M1-M4, independent work agrees with this repo that activation quantization does not pay: FastMetal-QAD built a W8A8 kernel and dropped it, and a lilting.ch study on an M1 Max found fp16 beating every int8 path it tried (review:97-99). The closest competitors are Siliconed, a standalone Swift and Metal app that streams the same Comfy-Org ConvRot files and runs Krea 2 at 1024² at about 18 s/step in 2.2 GB on an M1 Pro, against this repo's 14.9-15.1 s/step at 15 GB on an M1 Max (review:103; findings.md:331, :295), and Argus minimax-h3-mac, which streams H3 blocks to run 1344x768x124 at about 601 s per NFE on a 24 GB M4 Pro, where this repo is memory-bound at 1420-1940 s/step (review:104; findings.md:478). Both show that memory headroom, not packed decode, is the next lever (review:106). For Wan 2.2 5B at 1280x704, this repo's 37.4 s/step at 9.1 GB on an M1 Max compares with FastMetal's reported about 33 s/step at 9.3 GB on an M4 Max, while MLX on an M3 Max needs 56 GB for the same model (findings.md:152; review:109-110). No one else publishes per-step 4-bit or 6-bit H3 numbers on Apple GPUs (review:112).

## Limitations

1. **One machine.** Every number is from one Mac Studio with an M1 Max and 32 GB on torch 2.14.1 (README.md:5, :11). Nothing has been measured on M2, M3, M4 or M5, or on a different memory size. The MPS ratios are internally anchored, but there is no CUDA GEMM at the same shapes to anchor them externally (review:84).
2. **One prompt and one seed per model.** End-to-end runs use the same seed and are checked by visual inspection, absence of NaNs, and PSNR or SSIM where noted (findings.md:32, :546, :560). There is no LPIPS or CLIP score over a prompt set (review:62).
3. **No external baselines yet.** The repo has no mflux, stable-diffusion.cpp or Draw Things run on this machine, and MLX is not installed in the native environment (CONTRIBUTING.md:38; review:61). The comparisons in Related work are against published numbers from other chips.
4. **Most inference memory numbers are after-sampling snapshots, not peaks.** `bench/run_krea2.py` reads `driver_allocated_memory` once after the sampling loop and records it as `mps_alloc_gb_after_sample` (bench/run_krea2.py:164, :172). The findings tables label the column "GPU memory after sampling" (findings.md:34). The Wan 2.2 tables record sampling peaks (findings.md:150), and training peaks are driver peaks (findings.md:519-523). These are different measurements and should not be compared directly.
5. **bf16 emulation confounds the INT8-versus-bf16 comparisons on M1.** bf16 GEMM is about 1.75x slower than fp16 on this GPU (findings.md:21), so part of the training speed gap at findings.md:519-525 and part of the stock-versus-Metal gaps come from dtype, not from the kernel. The bf16 convolution slowdown is also measured only on this M1 Max (findings.md:100; review:71).
6. **Variance is thin.** Most numbers are one median from one process, with n=3-10 (review:59). The dense fp16 baseline drifts 2-4% across tables, which is the same size as the overhead claim.
7. **The Krea 2 1024² stock figure is noisy.** The 35 s stock number was taken during a download and is flagged for a rerun (findings.md:36, :45). The kernel's own 1024² figure has since moved from 16.5 to 14.9-15.1 s/step (findings.md:331, :295).
8. **Training evidence is bounded.** The one-block gradient check against an fp32 reference from Milestone 1 was never reported (lora-training note:77; review:65). The packed W4 and W6 backward is checked numerically only at small shapes, and the real-Qwen probe checks that gradients are finite and nonzero (findings.md:533; review:66). Cross-precision transfer of an adapter trained on INT8 to a bf16 base is an open question with no published test (lora-training note:67).
9. **Reproducibility.** The pinned fork commits at CONTRIBUTING.md:33-36 are not yet on the public forks, so a reviewer cannot rebuild the stack today (review:9, :15).

## Proposed aims and roadmap

Modal cannot test Metal. Its GPUs are NVIDIA, so it can supply CUDA anchors and fp64 oracles but nothing about Apple silicon (review:55, :143). Apple hardware with more memory is therefore the first budget line: an M4 or M5 machine with 64 GB or more, and access to at least one M2 and one M3 for the generation sweep. The experiment numbers below are the pre-grant review's E1-E13 (review:145-159).

**Aim 1: measure across Apple generations and memory sizes.** Run the kernel microbenchmarks (E2), the four-way Krea sweep (E1, stock, stock plus SDPA, Metal, Metal plus SDPA, fresh process each, five repeats, randomized order) and the wide-K control (E8) on M2, M3, M4 and M5, and on torch 2.15 where `_int_mm` now exists natively on MPS (review:44). On the M1 Max it is already measured: bit-exact, but 7 to 8 percent slower than fp16 `F.linear` at K=6144 and within noise of this repo's weight-only kernel (findings.md, 2026-11-09), so the open question is M3 and newer. Success: a table of kernel overhead against resident fp16 per chip with median, IQR and a bootstrap CI from three or more fresh processes, and a per-chip answer to whether weight-only still beats `_int_mm`. Cost: about 2 GPU-hours per chip for E1 plus hours for E2 and E8, and the hardware itself.

**Aim 2: anchor the numbers externally.** A CUDA GEMM at the same shapes on Modal (E4, under $1), bf16 reference renders for H3 and Wan 14B on an A100 (E3, about $15-25 of Modal credit), and matched recipes against mflux and stable-diffusion.cpp on the same Mac (E5, about 3 hours). Success: every headline ratio in this document has a cross-platform or cross-framework comparison next to it, and quality is reported with LPIPS and CLIP over a prompt set (E10, one day), not one seed.

**Aim 3: close the training gaps.** The gradient-fidelity probe, one block then two to four blocks, with dA and dB cosine and relative L2 for INT8, W6 and W4 against an fp32 dequant and a bf16 reference (E6, one to two days; the kernel arm on the Mac and the fp64 arm on Modal CPU), the packed W4 and W6 backward at real Qwen widths against fp64 (E7, hours), and the INT8-to-bf16 adapter transfer A/B the training note names as its Milestone 2 (lora-training note:79). Success: Milestone 1's criterion (1), a one-block gradient within 1e-3 of the fp32 reference (lora-training note:77), is reported for all three formats; W4 and W6 backward error is at the fp16 floor at K of 3072 and 12288; the transfer A/B is published either way.

**Aim 4: the next performance levers from the survey's Top 5.** The survey ranked them by speedup times feasibility (survey:49-55): step-reduction LoRAs (1.6-2x on Krea per image, days), a tiny-VAE draft path (decode from 74 s to seconds, about 60% of Wan 5B wall time, a day), the fused low-rank side path (exact, kills the merge, one to two weeks), a faster full-quality VAE plus fused adaLN and RoPE kernels (one to two weeks), and H3 compute reduction (two weeks, quality risk). The side path has since shipped (findings.md:127, :251). Of the rest, the ones with the clearest payoff are block streaming for H3 at 1344x768x124, with Argus's 601 s per NFE as the target to beat (E11, days), EasyCache on 8-step Krea and Qwen Turbo to close that question with data (E13, 20 minutes), and a re-check of the upstream verdicts on torch 2.15 and the 2.16 nightly (E12, hours). Success: H3 at native length under 601 s/step on a 32 GB machine without swapping; a yes-or-no on caching at 8 steps backed by a measured run; each upstream claim either re-confirmed or withdrawn on the current torch.

**Aim 5: land upstream and make the work reproducible.** Push the pinned branches to the public forks, update the pins, publish the GGUF node fork, and make docs/REPRODUCING.md runnable. It already describes a model-free kernel tier and a full-model tier, but neither can be run from public repositories until the pins are pushed (docs/REPRODUCING.md:6-17; review:15-20). Rebase comfy-kitchen #249 against the competing lane (review:131). Success: a reviewer on a clean Mac can run the model-free tier and reproduce the kernel-overhead table within the published CI, and at least one of the five upstream submissions has a human maintainer review.

The order is Aim 5, Aim 1, Aim 2, then Aims 3 and 4 in parallel. Aim 5 costs no new hardware and unblocks everything else. Aim 1 is where the budget goes.
