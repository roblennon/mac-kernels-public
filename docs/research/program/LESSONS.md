# Lessons for card writers and runners

Read this before writing or revising a card. The card shape that follows from it is [designs/TEMPLATE.md](designs/TEMPLATE.md). The dated record stays in [the checkpoint](checkpoint-2026-11-09.md).

**Gate the subject before testing the hypothesis.** Before the step that tests the hypothesis, confirm that the model, task, instrument or data has the property the test depends on, with room for the effect to show, against a bar written in advance, with a stop. A planted-effect check does not count, because it tests the instrument, not the subject. Copies-as-evidence step 1a passed every measuring check in the lab's case 002 and still decided nothing: with thinking off, the 1.7B model got no greedy answer right, so the hypothesis was never tested. Evidence: "Result of step 1a" in [copies-as-evidence](designs/copies-as-evidence.md); the checkpoint's "What was learned"; commit 34c2cb6, which added gates to the other queued cards.

**Reviewers check what the artifact names, so the review prompt must name the subject question.** Four review passes and four cold reads of copies-as-evidence checked the measurement and never asked whether the model could do the base task. The fix is the question, not more reviewers. Every attack and cold-read prompt asks it outright. Evidence: the copies-as-evidence review record; the lab's process log.

**Set tolerances above the format's own error.** Derive the error the numeric format makes by itself (bf16 rounding, accumulation order) first, then set each check's tolerance above it. Copies-as-evidence's first identity stop of 0.1 nat sat below the card's own bf16 bound, and the library reference later failed on bf16 rounding at p near 0.5. Evidence: the copies-as-evidence review record; commits e3ae72b and c252872.

**A reading has one source.** The card's result section for that run is the only place a reading of it lives. Messages point to it rather than restate numbers, and a correction lists every place the old reading went. The example: the reading that the model was "at chance" on the sum (−2.4 nats per digit against −2.3) averaged digits that behave differently. In fact one template was inaccurate and the other broke format. Before the card was corrected, the phrase had reached the lab's case 002 outcome record (lab commit 3af165f), the lesson text given to about 30 sweep agents, and the review records of about 22 cards. Every copy in this repository was corrected on 2026-11-10; the lab's three copies are in its own cleanup list. Evidence: "Result of step 1a" in [copies-as-evidence](designs/copies-as-evidence.md).

**A card holds the current design, not its history.** When a review round changes a card, rewrite the affected text in place and add one dated line of at most two sentences to the review record. Copies-as-evidence reached 15,176 words, with a "Step 1, exactly" of about 9,000 and review entries of 300 to 500 words each, because every round appended and none removed. Evidence: `wc -w designs/*.md`; the copies-as-evidence review record.

**A threshold changed after its check fails is a plan change.** Record the failing values, and have the lab's skeptic look before the plan is rebound to the new card commit. The p 0.9 floor for the library reference was proposed after that check failed in the second pilot and accepted the same minute (13:35 UTC). Evidence: commit c252872.

**Search fan-out runs three agents at a time, against a budget of about 200 calls per lead turn.** Web search is shared across every subagent of a lead turn. About 30 search agents launched at once drained it within minutes, twice in one session, and most cards' first search gate had to be redone. Evidence: the checkpoint's "What was learned"; the decision in [TASKS.md](TASKS.md); Slice A in [FRAME.md](FRAME.md).

**Cross-family review earns its place at queue and design level.** A reviewer from another model family read the queue and its top cards after four Claude passes. It found a wrong confidence bound (no events in N draws bounds p at about 3/N, not 1/N²), an identification argument in img-subliminal that could not separate its two claims, and a queue top that repeated one operational bet. Keep it there. It did not catch the subject gap either, which is the lesson above, not a reason to add passes. Evidence: [the queue review](reviews/2026-11-09-queue-review-sol.md); the checkpoint's "What was learned".

**Read a gate from the run you were going to do anyway.** A subject gate that needs its own harness can cost more than it saves. Where a card already plans a baseline run, the gate is read from that run, first, before any other arm. A separate gate batch is worth it only for the cheapest cards. Count harness-writing time, downloads and model loads alongside device minutes when judging that. Evidence: [Sol's gate review](reviews/2026-11-10-gate-review-sol.md), section 4.

**A gate's bar is judged by how often it fails a subject that is fine.** Several gates first written on 2026-11-10 would have failed good subjects half the time or more: four separate 11-of-16 bars pass a 75 percent reader only 43 percent of the time, and an 8-of-10 bar passes a 70 percent subject 38 percent of the time. Pool trials, read room in the continuous score rather than in accuracy ceilings, and allow one bounded confirmation for borderline results. Evidence: the same review, section 1.

**A speedup is quoted on the workload, not on the benchmark that shows the mechanism.** A benchmark built to exercise a mechanism shows that it works and how large it can get. The number that predicts use comes from replaying real requests. n-gram drafting ahead of MTP decoded a whole-file edit 2.8 times faster on the Kaggle T4 but agent traffic only 1.16 times faster, because the agent's edits were short diffs and its reasoning had nothing to copy. A card that claims a speedup names the workload it was measured on, and runs a real-traffic replay before any claim about use. Evidence: "Result (2026-11-10)" in [the decode thread](threads/mps-decode.md); `kaggle-qwen-serve` EVIDENCE.md, the two 2026-11-10 entries.

**One process is a hint, not a number.** A speed claim needs several processes with the arms interleaved, every sample kept, and an A/A pair: the same pipeline twice, so its gap shows the noise. In one process, verifying 4 drafts cost 1.19x a single step; across four interleaved processes it cost 1.52x. A 9% gap between two nominally identical compiled pipelines turned out to be the A/A noise at batch 1. Evidence: the findings log, "Decode rerun under fixed rules" (2026-11-10).

**Time the steady state, and know what the step before did.** On the M1 Max, the first forward after a long compute-bound phase (a 4,096-token prefill) pays about 30 ms once, because memory-bound work runs slowly for tens of milliseconds afterwards. A harness that re-prefilled before every sample timed that penalty instead of the step. It made 4k decoding look twice as slow as it is, diluted the attention cliff, and voided a prediction. Rewind the cache counter between samples (`bench_verify_cost.py --rewind`), and report the first step after a prefill as its own number. Evidence: the findings log, "A small-query attention kernel removes the 9-row cliff" (2026-11-11).

**Review the harness before it spends the GPU.** A cross-family review of the rerun harness, before any run, found three blockers:
- failed checks did not stop the run;
- one planted control would crash before giving its verdict;
- the rollback check could pass without covering every case.

The earlier review, after the run, could only withdraw numbers. Once results exist, a review can only discount them; before, it can fix what produces them. Evidence: [the harness review](reviews/2026-11-10-rerun-harness-review-sol.md); [the decode-stack review](reviews/2026-11-10-decode-stack-review-sol.md).
