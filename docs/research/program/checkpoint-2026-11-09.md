## What was learned

- The obvious version of almost any quantization idea is taken in 2026, often by a low-visibility Zenodo record or an OpenReview submission. What stays open is the control nobody ran or the measurement someone named as future work.
- A single drafting-and-attack pass does not make a card runnable. Every one of the top five failed its first cold read with ten or more open decisions. A cold read by a fresh agent is the check that matters for "can someone run this tomorrow".
- A cross-family reviewer caught things four Claude passes had not: a wrong statistical bound, a broken identification argument, and a queue whose top repeated one operational bet.
- The first card the lab ran (copies-as-evidence, 2026-11-10) came back void for a reason no review caught: the 1.7B model with thinking off was at chance on the base task, so there was no accuracy for the replayed error to take away. Every measurement check passed. Drafting, attack, search and four cold reads all checked the instrument and never the subject. A card now needs a capability gate before its hypothesis test: does the model, task or data have the property the test depends on, with room for the effect to show? The first card is being redesigned around one, and every other queued card was checked for the same gap.
- Web search for subagents is capped at about 200 calls per turn of the lead session. Thirty agents searching at once drained it in minutes and left most cards with an unrun gate. Three at a time worked.
